A bank statement in the format Yardi actually imports, a matching ledger export, and a reconciliation that ties to exactly zero — built so every gap in the teardown has a concrete example sitting in it.

The short answer on formats: for this product, in the United States, the standard is BAI2. It is what Yardi's own automatic bank reconciliation imports, and it is what you should design the intake screen around.

The modern international standard is ISO 20022 camt.053, and it is where everything is heading — but US property banking still runs on a flat file from 1987.

One number in your demo is a problem

Your product claims a 96% first-pass match rate.

Published guidance on Yardi's own automatic bank reconciliation puts typical auto-clear rates at 60–80% after optimisation. Not on day one — after tuning.

So your headline figure sits roughly twenty points above what the incumbent achieves on the same job. Anyone who has run this process will notice, and the question that follows — "on what data?" — currently has no good answer, because the number came from a seed file.

Two honest ways out. Either drop the figure into the believable band and label it as illustrative, or — much stronger — keep a real number and say where it came from: "78% on a month I reconciled by hand first." The dataset below is the beginning of being able to say that.

A modest number you can defend beats an impressive one you can't. This is the single fastest credibility win available to you.

The landscape

What a statement actually arrives as

Four formats matter. Only one of them is the right primary target for this product today.

BAI2

Design for this

A flat text file of numbered record types, published by the Bank Administration Institute in 1987 and maintained today by the standards body ASC X9. Dominant in US corporate banking, and the format Yardi's automatic bank reconciliation is built around. Every transaction carries a numeric type code saying what kind of movement it was — which is exactly the signal your matcher needs.

camt.053

Design for this next

The ISO 20022 end-of-day statement, in XML. Much richer and properly structured rather than free text, which is why it produces higher automatic match rates. It is the international direction of travel and the replacement for MT940. Worth supporting second — and worth naming in an interview, because it shows you know where the ground is moving.

MT940

Fading

The old SWIFT statement message — tagged fields with transaction details crammed into free text, which is precisely why it matches badly. Being superseded by camt.053. Support it if a customer needs it; don't build around it.

CSV, OFX, and PDF

The real world

Yardi supports OFX and CSV with extra configuration, and plenty of smaller banks send nothing better than a PDF. This is the mess that justifies a model in your Reader — but note that the bigger the customer, the more likely they already have BAI2.

One finding that should become a design object

Type codes are not fully standard. Banks vary in which codes they use, and publish their own proprietary ones — the 900s especially. So "code 555 means a returned deposit" is a per-bank mapping, not a universal truth.

Your product currently has nowhere to keep that. It should: a per-bank code dictionary, owned by the administrator, versioned, and recorded against each run. That's a real object you're missing, and it's the sort of thing that only turns up when you look at the actual files.

What the incumbent does

Yardi already works the way the teardown said it should

This is the most useful thing the research turned up, and it's a strong validation of the fixes — the system you're posting into already thinks in exactly these terms.

Yardi's bank reconciliation is a three-column view: a ledger column, a bank column, and a difference column. The bank side is adjusted for outstanding checks and deposits in transit. The difference must reach zero before anything can be posted.

That is the balance proof from Gap 4, and it is not optional in the target system. A product that posts to Yardi without producing it is producing an input that Yardi itself would reject.

Yardi also splits corrections into two kinds, and the words are worth stealing:

  • Book reconciling items — the ledger is wrong and needs an entry. Bank fees, interest, the bounced payment.
  • Bank reconciling items — the bank is wrong and needs correcting.

Those are your resolution verbs from Gap 3, already named by the system you're integrating with. Using the customer's vocabulary rather than inventing your own is free credibility.

And the matching itself: Yardi matches on amount, date and reference number. That is a deterministic rule ladder — no model — which is exactly the argument in the agents piece, made by the incumbent.

Four adjusting entries are needed, and the month is closed. Note that the returned payment and its fee together reverse the original rent exactly — which is why treating them as one event rather than three mysteries is the right design.

Notice also what the ambiguous refund does. Whichever of the two $210 rows the reviewer picks, the other one becomes an outstanding item carried into June. The choice isn't cosmetic — it changes what next month starts with. That's the clearest possible argument for why Gap 1 matters.

What you have

Two files, and what to do with them

bai2-westlake-operating-2026-05.bai

A well-formed BAI2 statement — header, account balances, fourteen transactions, and trailers whose control totals actually reconcile. Drop this into a screen mock-up and the intake step stops being imaginary.

yardi-gl-westlake-operating-2026-05.csv

Sixteen ledger rows in a Yardi-shaped export: property code, account code, post date and post month, batch and control references, check number, payee, debit and credit columns, and a cleared flag. The post-month column is where period close lives.

Three things to do with them

Reconcile the month by hand, once. An hour with a spreadsheet. You'll understand this domain better afterwards than any amount of reading achieves, and you'll be able to defend every claim in the case study because you'll have done the job.

Redraw the screens against real rows. Designs made from invented data hide problems — the description field that's 60 characters of shouting capitals, the check number that isn't there, the reference that repeats across three transactions. Real files surface those immediately.

Use it as your one honest measurement. Reconcile it by hand, then say what a matcher would have got right. That's a defensible number with a stated source, which is worth more than any percentage you could invent.

Why this matters more than it looks

Grounded beats invented

The strongest thing in this whole research pass isn't the file format. It's that Yardi's own reconciliation already works in three columns that must reach zero, already splits corrections into book and bank items, and already matches on amount, date and reference. Every significant fix in the teardown turns out to be how the incumbent already behaves — which means they weren't opinions. They were the domain, and you can now say so with a source.
